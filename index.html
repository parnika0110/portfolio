About — the person behind the commits

Learning by building, then figuring out why it broke.

I turn “what if?” into useful software — practical AI tools, dependable backends, and products people can actually use.

I'm a final-year Computer Science Engineering student at Jyothy Institute of Technology, Bengaluru, currently holding a 9.32 CGPA. Most of what I know, I've learned by building — rental platforms, gesture-controlled dashboards, recipe recommenders — and then figuring out why they broke.

I care about software engineering that's practical: systems that hold up, backends that make sense, and interfaces people actually enjoy using. Lately I've been spending more time with AI-powered applications and cloud tooling, and I like that there's always another layer to learn.

AI Opportunity Discovery Platform

OPPY

An AI-powered platform that helps students and early-career users discover, save and track opportunities.

Demoed at D-Day, Bengaluru Tech Week 2026 ↓

Problem
Internships, jobs, hackathons, fellowships, events, grants and scholarships are scattered across the web — and finding, personalising and tracking them happens in different places.
What I built
One platform that discovers real opportunities across the web and matches them to each user: natural-language and voice search (Sarvam AI), “Recommended for you” picks, a saved list and application tracking.

Gesture Controlled Infotainment System

A touch-free infotainment dashboard driven by real-time hand gesture recognition.

Problem
Controlling music, navigation, calls and settings on an infotainment dashboard without touching the screen.

OpenCV captures webcam frames, MediaPipe Hands tracks hand landmarks, the index fingertip drives a pointer and a thumb–index pinch triggers actions — plus map-based navigation.

AI & Weekends — D-Day

From building to presenting.

I was selected as one of 52 builders from over 300 applicants for AI & Weekends' Back to School builder cohort, a 15-day sprint focused on turning ideas into working products. At the end of the programme, five builders were selected to showcase their products at D-Day during Bengaluru Tech Week 2026, where I demoed OPPY to founders, builders and the Bengaluru tech community.

Looking back

OPPY started from one idea — good opportunities shouldn’t be this hard to find. The cohort’s fifteen days were about turning that into something people could actually use, and D-Day was where I got to share it with founders, builders and the Bengaluru tech community. Seeing the other cohort projects alongside mine was a good reminder of how much I learn from the people building around me.
